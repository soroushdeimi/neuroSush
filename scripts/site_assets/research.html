Grid cells and TEM: what we studied and what we built

An exhaustive look at entorhinal spatial representations, Whittington et al. 2020 (TEM) and Kawahara & Fujisawa 2025 (Spiking TEM), our rate-coded and spiking re-implementations, stability fixes, and spatial evaluation toolkit.

01 / BACKGROUND

Grid Cells in One Screen

Grid cells are specialized neurons located in the medial entorhinal cortex (MEC) that fire at multiple locations forming a periodic, hexagonal lattice tiling the environment. Discovered in rodents and humans, they provide a metric, internal coordinate system for physical and abstract space.

Why they matter: they suggest that the brain computes spatial relationships and relational structures using low-dimensional manifold representations. The core question we study is whether neural network models trained to learn relational structure shared across diverse environments organically develop grid-like representations or whether spatial periodicity requires structural constraints.

02 / LITERATURE REVIEW

The Two Papers We Studied

Whittington et al. 2020 (Cell 183) — Tolman-Eichenbaum Machine (TEM)

Question: How does the hippocampal-entorhinal system maintain and generalize cognitive maps across environments?

Architecture: Factorisation of relational structure g (where am I on the graph) and sensory identity x (what do I see), combined into conjunctive representation p = g ⊙ x, stored in a fast Hebbian memory M reset per world, while slow transition weights (graph dynamics) are learned across worlds via backpropagation.

What they report: Generalisation of predictive inference to novel routes in new environments; emergence of grid-like rate maps and place-like fields in factorised units.

Limits: Relies on low-rank graph approximations and idealized training schedules; evaluated primarily on abstract graph worlds.

Kawahara & Fujisawa 2025 (bioRxiv) — Spiking TEM

Question: Can spiking neural networks implement TEM principles in biologically plausible dynamics?

Architecture: TensorFlow 2 spiking network implementation featuring spiking neuron models and a theta oscillation term in SpikingDense.

What they report: Implementation of spiking TEM dynamics and spatial representation analyses.

Limits: In our public code audit (one run, 40k iterations, 8x8 worlds), prediction loss remained near chance (2.1-2.5); spatial probes show strong within-world position tuning (CA1 spatial info 2.614) but no evidence of shared cross-world structure (map corr near zero, RSA near zero), population distances follow sensory identity rather than grid distance, gridness is unresolvable at 8x8, and no phase precession machinery is present.
