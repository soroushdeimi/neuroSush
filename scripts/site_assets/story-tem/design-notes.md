# Same map, new world: design notes

The journey follows a spatial memory from world to circuit, into spike timing, and back to changed worlds. The arena is the recurring anchor. Place fields introduce the meaning of an activity map before the camera follows entorhinal and hippocampal pathways. Timing, structural binding, and prediction provide different views of the same memory problem. The closing arena returns the reader to the opening question. Camera zoom is an authored focus change, not a calibrated physical scale.

The page adapts the neuron story’s architecture: one fixed canvas, one SVG overlay, one persistent camera and one normalized scroll target. Neighboring scenes crossfade while the camera interpolates. Animation time runs separately from scroll position so readers can inspect a mechanism without advancing the prose. Pause settles the camera at its target. Navigation supports buttons, arrows, Page Up/Down, Home and End. A new panel resets its reading position. The scroll track scales with panel count.

Site tokens supply typography, colors, contrast and light/dark themes. Desktop reserves separate text and drawing regions. Phone layout places the drawing above the text. The reading region scrolls when necessary, with navigation outside it. Native selects and a timeline control select recorded variants, worlds and positions. Source references remain available in the reduced-motion version.

SCHEMATIC means authored teaching geometry. It includes idealized LIF voltage, theta windows, STDP timing, field formation, remapping, and circuit dependencies. These are not trained networks running in the browser. Numerical result charts read the reported measurements from the fact ledger. No empirical learning trajectory is fabricated from the animated field formation. No physical playback speed is claimed.

REPLAY means the path and whole-walk activity maps come from the published local recordings. Signed structural activity is displayed as signed activity, not a firing rate or bits per spike. Maps summarize the whole walk and do not change with the instantaneous path marker. The recordings do not support a claim of learned grid cells. The six-run control margins come only from the project’s results report, with its upper-tail and small-world caveats.

The ideal square/hexagonal illustration is OUR COMPUTATION, independent of trained models and of the paper’s unavailable supplementary scoring definition. The generator uses the library’s standard hexagonal score and existing reference-test geometry: 49 bins per side, scale 10 bins, annulus 5–15 lag bins, zero orientation and double precision. The score is minimum correlation at 60°/120° minus maximum at 30°/90°/150°. Tests independently reconstruct the ideal fields and recompute the scores from the ledger’s stored maps. These sampling choices are examples, not universal scores for each lattice family.

The generated ledger is the single source of displayed scientific values. Its entries distinguish paper claims, definitions, our reported results, and our separate computation. All displayed claims use VERIFIED facts. The unresolved square-grid scoring critique stays NOT VERIFIED and is omitted. The preprint’s DG counts, approximate Figure 7 reading, and printed equation ambiguities are described as reporting issues without guessing an intended correction. No paper figure is cropped or reused.

Reduced motion renders a fixed explanatory moment for each panel and supplies the full copy, caveat and references as ordinary page content. It does not start a replay or animation. The stills preserve the same provenance distinctions.

Validation serves the built site over plain HTTP. Desktop and CDP-emulated phone screenshots cover every panel in both themes, plus reduced motion. Browser checks include runtime and console errors, failed requests, overflow, panel/navigation overlap, pause, keyboard/button navigation, source-dialog dismissal and replay controls. Reported frame rates are short headless-browser measurements on the host, not measurements from a physical phone.
